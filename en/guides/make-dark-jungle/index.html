GUIDE_09 / DARK JUNGLE

How to make dark jungle in Suno: chopped breaks over a slow, heavy sub

Fast drums over a slow floor: the whole genre lives in that tension. This guide assembles the prompt block by block and names what tends to leak in.

What defines dark, degraded jungle

Jungle inverts the usual hierarchy. In most genres the drums accompany something; here the chopped break is the lead instrument and everything else clears space for it. A dark jungle prompt spends its best words on the break: what it is cut from, how hard it is chopped, how worn it sounds.

The second trait is the split tempo. The surface runs fast — chopped breaks at drum and bass speed — while the sub underneath holds long, slow notes. That tension between fast drums and a slow floor is the engine of the genre. Lose it and you get one of two worse things: a breakbeat loop with no weight, or a bass track with busy drums.

The dark variant adds two more decisions. The sources sound old: tape, vinyl, resampled generations, edges worn off. And the space comes from dub — echoes that swallow a hat or a snare and decay into the dark — not from a clean hall or a bright plate.

Building the prompt block by block

Assemble the prompt from four blocks: genre, break, sub, surface. One working line: dark jungle, chopped resampled break, heavy sustained sub, dusty tape texture, dub echo throws, instrumental. Every block is a decision, and the order puts the genre word first so the tempo default lands before anything else.

The break block deserves more words than any other. chopped break, resampled, rearranged, dusty names the source family and the treatment; the drums guide covers why source words do more than impact adjectives. The sub block states the opposition outright: deep sustained sub, slow bass movement under fast drums. Keeping that sub audible without inflating it is the subject of the low-end guide.

The surface block carries the dark variant. Degradation words — tape hiss, vinyl crackle, worn sample — plus one dub-space phrase: dub delay on the snare, dark cavernous space. One phrase of each family is enough.

The axes worth varying between attempts

Thirty beats from one scheme is a matter of axes, not thirty new ideas. Keep the genre word and the hierarchy fixed: break in front, sub underneath. Change one axis per attempt.

  • Chop depth. From looped break, light chops to heavily chopped, rearranged break. The far end turns the break into new material; the near end keeps the original groove readable.
  • Drum density. Constant motion on one side; half-time section, sparse stepper pattern on the other. The same break reads differently at each density.
  • Sub behavior. Held notes — long static sub notes — against movement: sliding sub, slow dub bassline.
  • Degradation depth. From light vinyl dust to crushed tape, heavily degraded. Each step trades clarity for age.
  • Echo amount. dry chops, occasional echo throw at one end; drowned in dub delay at the other.

Typical exclusions for this genre

The tool associates fast breaks with modern drum and bass, and modern drum and bass is clean. That association leaks in as polished drums, bright wide mixes and synth bass with teeth. The Exclude field is where you cut it off; the negative prompts guide covers how that field reads.

Three leaks account for most spoiled attempts. Name them in the Exclude field.

  • Modern DnB polish. modern dnb, clean mastering, bright mix keeps the production in the era of the sources.
  • Neurofunk brightness. neurofunk, aggressive mid bass, metallic bass blocks the screeching midrange that replaces a sub with a lead.
  • EDM furniture. risers, build-ups, big drop, supersaw removes the arrangement clichés that fast tempos attract.

Common failure modes

Dark jungle fails in recognizable ways. Each one traces back to a block that was left vague.

  • The break comes back programmed. Fast machine drums instead of a sampled break means the source words lost to the genre default. Put chopped break early, add treatment words and drop any drum-machine vocabulary.
  • The bass runs at drum speed. A busy bassline under fast drums erases the genre's tension. Write the opposition into the prompt: slow heavy sub under fast breaks.
  • Everything degrades at once. Global dirt words blur the whole mix. Aim the dirt at the sources and keep the floor clean: dusty breaks, clean deep sub.
  • The tempo drifts. A halfway result — slow break, hip-hop pocket — usually means the genre word got buried. Keep it first, and keep the neighboring genres in Exclude.
