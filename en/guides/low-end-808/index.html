GUIDE_04 / LOW END

808, sub-bass and movement: controlling the low end in a Suno prompt

The low end is where generated tracks fall apart first. These are the words that decide whether you get a sub, an 808 or a bassline, and how to keep them out of each other's way.

Sub, 808 or bassline: different words select different things

The tool does not treat "bass" as one instrument; it is a family. sub-bass or deep sub tends to select a low, sustained, near-sine layer with almost no harmonics — felt more than heard, no attack. 808 selects a tuned drum: an instrument with a transient, a decay and, in most genres, a melodic role. bassline, bass guitar or synth bass select a note-playing instrument with most of its energy in the mids, where small speakers can hear it. mid bass pushes energy up into the growl range and away from the sub.

Written alone, deep bass is ambiguous, and the tool resolves the ambiguity with the genre default. If the style says trap, you get an 808. If it says dub, you get a bassline. Naming the exact layer is the cheapest fix in the whole prompt: deep sustained sub-bass and long 808 glides are different requests, and they come back different.

Movement: holding, sliding, walking

The instrument word decides the timbre; the movement word decides what it does over eight bars. A static sub holds one note per chord and works as a floor: static sustained sub-bass, minimal movement. A sliding 808 changes pitch on the way between notes: sliding 808 glides between notes. A walking or dub-style bassline plays a phrase: slow melodic dub bassline, space between notes.

Skip the movement word and the tool picks one for you, and it usually picks busy. Bass that never stops moving eats the space the drums need. Deciding between holding, sliding and walking before you write the prompt is faster than regenerating until the low end calms down.

Kick and bass: decide who owns the low end

Only one element can own the fundamental. In trap that element is the 808, so the kick gets described as short and functional: short punchy kick, 808 carries the low end. Over a sustained sub, the opposite applies: the kick needs enough attack to read through the floor, something like hard kick cutting through a deep sub.

You can also describe the relationship itself. Words like ducking, pumping or bass ducks under the kick push the generation toward a sidechain feel: the sub breathes with the kick instead of fighting it. What you cannot do is describe both as the biggest thing in the mix. Two elements asked to dominate the same octave cancel into mud.

A muddy low end: where it comes from and how to clean it

"Deep heavy massive bass" next to "lush warm pads" is a recipe, and the dish is mud. Every adjective in that pair pushes energy into the same low-mid region, and the tool has no mix engineer to carve it back out. A muddy low end is rarely a bass problem; it is an everything-at-once problem stated at prompt level.

The fix has two halves. In the style prompt, assign lowness to one element and dryness to the rest: heavy sub, dry drums, sparse arrangement leaves room by construction. In the Exclude field, name the failure directly: muddy low end, boomy bass, dense pads, washy reverb. How exclusions work, and why they belong in their own field, is covered in the negative prompts guide.

Genre defaults: each genre wants a different low end

"808" is not one sound across genres, so describe the behaviour, not just the name. A trap 808 is the lead: tuned, gliding, louder than everything else, with the drums kept dry around it. That is the behaviour Trap Ritual is written around: 30 prompts where the 808 dominates and the kit stays out of its way.

A garage sub does the opposite job. It sits still underneath the 2-step swing, warm and static, while the drums carry all the syncopation — the pattern used across Garaje Oscuro. A dub bass is huge but slow: melodic, round, wrapped in echo, with the kick landing inside it rather than against it, which is the register Abyss Dub works in. Writing dominant gliding 808, static warm sub under 2-step drums or slow melodic dub bass, deep echoes asks for each behaviour on purpose instead of inheriting the genre default.

Common mistakes

Most low-end failures in generated tracks come from the same short list. Pick the layer, pick the movement, pick the owner of the low end: three decisions, three or four words each.

  • Writing deep heavy massive bass next to dense pads and expecting the tool to make room.
  • Asking for an 808 when you want a sustained sub, or the reverse.
  • Describing both kick and bass as the dominant element at once.
  • Leaving movement unspecified, so the genre default decides whether the bass holds, slides or walks.
  • Stacking size adjectives instead of naming one behaviour and one owner of the low end.
